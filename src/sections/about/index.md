As the Adapted Technology Specialist at CSM, I provide students with access to technologies that level the playing field and allow them to perform to their capabilities. In addition, especially through my support of the Alternate Media program, adapted technology services can become the last line of defense to ensure the student's learning materials are accessible and available for use with their assistive technologies. This dual responsibility to ensure access to technologies & to ensure they work with the students' instructional materials leads me to work closely with faculty & staff to raise awareness about the importance of accessible instructional materials and which technologies may be used to access them.

If I had to try and put this approach into a teaching philosophy, I think it's fair to say that I believe that "access comes first". This is especially relevant in the context of technology use in classrooms today as the practice of statistical pattern generation is being scrutinized by the educational community. Assistive technologies have long relied on decoding diverse media and interfaces as part of disability-specific machine translation and have embraced the new possibilities from greater data availability leading to more generalized capabilities. On the other hand, educators are protective of pedagogy's central role in fostering critical reflection among students and are questioning the technologies as assistants framing associated with the latest generation of machine intelligence.

As Adapted Technology Specialist I hope to contribute to a nuanced discussion of mediated assistance in service of access and participation by listening to students, faculty and staff alike and working to come to a shared understanding on an individual basis.
