I am privileged to primarily experience students one-on-one, often even as one of their first contacts on campus. I prioritize letting students set the pace, listening for the barriers they encounter, and making space for them to describe what would make learning more workable.

My role is not to prescribe the most impressive tool. It is to understand what is getting in the way for this student, in this course, on this device, and then support an informed choice. I use thoughtful questions, demonstrations, and practice to help students compare options and decide what fits their goals, routines, and learning environment.

## Start with access, lead toward learning

Technology instruction begins with access, but it must lead to learning. I demonstrate tools in the context of actual class tasks and help students practice the complete workflow: capturing information, organizing it, revising it, and retrieving it later. An audio recording, transcript, or automated summary can provide a valuable safety net, but it is not by itself a study strategy. I teach students to turn accessible formats into active learning through review, retrieval, elaboration, and spaced practice.

## Teach the trade-offs

Adapted technology is shaped by more than features. Privacy, accuracy, cost, policy, transparency, and the realities of a student's digital environment all affect whether a tool is genuinely useful. With AI-enabled tools, I make those trade-offs visible and ask where data goes, how the system works, and when human review remains necessary. This helps students become critical users rather than passive recipients of technology.

## Build independence through partnership

My goal is a student who can explain why a tool helps, adapt the workflow to a new class, advocate for what they need, and use technology with increasing independence. I also collaborate with faculty and colleagues on accessible course materials, alternate media, and shared practices so that responsibility for access does not rest solely on the student.

I use student feedback, observation, and professional dialogue to refine instruction and the supports surrounding it. Success is not simply that a student can operate a tool during an appointment; it is that the tool becomes a sustainable part of their learning process and expands their ability to participate.
