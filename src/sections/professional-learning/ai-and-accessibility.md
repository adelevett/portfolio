## The question

AI tools arrived in students' workflows whether institutions were ready or not. My inquiry focuses on two questions: where can these tools genuinely reduce access barriers for students with disabilities, and what guardrails make that use responsible?

## What this looks like in practice

Data policy work comes first. I've contributed to institutional thinking about what student data may and may not flow through AI API services, because the privacy question has to be answered before the tool question.

With students, I introduce tools such as NotebookLM inside structured follow-ups, with explicit attention to privacy and appropriate use. For alternate media, I continue exploring AI-assisted PDF, ePub, and text-to-speech processes that support timely, usable materials, documented in [AI-assisted workflows](/2026/spring/noat/). And through workgroup and research conversations with colleagues across the district, I keep comparing notes on where these tools help and where they don't.

The throughline: emerging tools earn a place in student support only when they survive the same test as everything else. Do they fit the person, the course, and the task?
