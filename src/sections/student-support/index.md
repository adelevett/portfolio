I meet with students to understand the barrier first, then identify and practice a sustainable technology workflow. Follow-up is part of the work: a tool is only useful when it fits the student's course, device, and way of learning.

Since joining the DRC, this has meant hundreds of one-on-one consultations and structured follow-up messages, so that no student leaves with a tool they haven't rehearsed on their own coursework.

## Adapted Technology Consultations

Adapted technology can include specialized tools, built-in accessibility features, and thoughtfully assembled workflows. I work with students to identify options specific to their disability-related needs and academic goals, then make sure the choice holds up in practice.

### How the work runs

**Consultation.** Understand the barrier, the courses, the devices, and what the student has already tried.

**Setup and training.** Configure the tool together and rehearse it against real coursework, not a demo file.

**Structured follow-up.** Every student receives a written follow-up summarizing their setup, with steps they can reuse later.

### Tools in regular rotation

- Note-taking: smartpens, Otter AI, Jamworks, audio-supported note workflows
- Reading and text-to-speech: Natural Reader, Kurzweil, built-in screen readers and speak features
- Writing: speech-to-text including Dragon, dictation, and writing supports
- Everyday accessibility: the features already on students' phones, tablets, and laptops, often the fastest win
- Emerging tools: guided, policy-aware use of AI study tools such as NotebookLM, with attention to what data students share

### What I've learned

The most durable outcomes come from modest tools practiced well. Students rarely need the most advanced option; they need one workflow that survives week twelve of the semester.

## Alternate Media

Students with qualifying print disabilities may receive learning materials in alternate formats. In partnership with the Alternate Media Specialist, I help prepare course materials in formats students can actually use, and train students to access those materials with confidence.

### The workflow

- **Coordination of alternate-format requests** from intake through delivery, including status tracking during high-volume periods like the start of term and winter break
- **Accessible PDF and ePub workflows**, including review of scans, readings, textbooks, and course files
- **Publisher and library sources**, including Bookshare, when a ready-made accessible edition exists
- **Student training** so converted materials are opened, navigated, and listened to effectively with tools like Natural Reader and Kurzweil

### Working with faculty

Alternate media is also a faculty-facing service. I respond to textbook and course-material questions, help faculty understand what makes a source file convertible, and flag accessibility issues before they reach students. The earlier a course file is fixed, the fewer emergency conversions happen at week one.
